# Product lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **It starts where the customer already is.** A list sent on WhatsApp needs no app, no account, nothing to learn. Right first five minutes.
- **The ending is local and easy to feel:** a bag in your own village instead of a drive down to Jbeil. One trip replaces many.
- **One village keeps every seam visible,** and "it runs in my own village" is the only believable way to sell the software later.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The first wrong bag.** A customer sends "2 kilo tomatoes, labneh, the big Nido", maybe as a voice note or in Arabizi. The parser must turn that into exact products and sizes. The driver finds the size gone or the offer over. Who picks the substitute? The customer is not in the aisle, the driver cannot wait for replies, and a stranger is choosing their tomatoes. The pitch is silent here, yet this *is* the product. One wrong item plus a surprise total, and the household goes back to driving down.
2. **No focus: the pitch is a feature list.** Pooling, a scraping agent across 5–6 stores, split batches, dashboard, driver view, licensing. Each extra store adds a stop, a separate bag per household, and a website price that may not match the shelf. Pooling adds an allocation problem: three households each want one of a "buy 3" item; whose invoice gets the discount? Designed from the software out, not back from the bag.
3. **Money and the pickup handover.** Dollar cash, wallets, totals that change after substitutions, and a cash-light business fronting the whole batch at the till. Then the bag waits: who hands it over, what hours, where do chilled and frozen items sit, what happens to an uncollected bag or a disputed bill? With the fee undecided, the customer cannot be told what they will pay.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence: what does the customer get, by when, at what price?
- Show me the WhatsApp message a customer gets when an item is out of stock, and when it arrives.
- When the parser is unsure, who checks, and how many orders fit between 2 p.m. and the driver leaving?
- When is the customer's price fixed: at order, at the till, or at pickup?
- Who staffs the pickup point, what hours, what fridge?
- How does the receipt prove it beat going down? Shelf prices: **/founder-competitors**.
- How many households, how often, what basket, what fee? **/founder-consumer**. Does the saving survive fee, driver, fuel, packaging? **/founder-cfo**.
- What share of lines will need a substitute? Nobody knows; only a pilot answers it.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Write the one-sentence promise first** and build only what it needs, e.g. "List by 2 p.m., a checked bag in Amchit the same evening, town prices plus a fixed fee."
2. **One store for the pilot.** No comparison agent until real shelf prices show a second store saves more than the extra stop costs (/founder-competitors, then /founder-cfo).
3. **A substitution rule before the first order:** each item marked "similar is fine / skip / ask me"; one confirmation with an estimated total before the driver leaves; a final receipt flagging every change.
4. **Run it by hand first:** WhatsApp, a spreadsheet, the founder driving, a small fixed group of households. Track bags exactly right, lines substituted, checking minutes per order, repeat orders. Set the pass mark first; code only what hurts.
5. **Settle payment and pickup before launch:** who fronts cash, how customers pay, a named person and hours, cold storage, a rule for uncollected bags.
