# Monopoly lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small market it could own first.** One village, one pickup point, one daily run: a big share of something tiny. The founder lives there, so first customers arrive by WhatsApp and word of mouth almost free.
- **Density is a real local scale effect.** A driver run costs about the same carrying few orders or many, so each extra household lowers cost per order, and a second entrant is left splitting thin volume. The one structural advantage here.
- **Timing.** Post-2019 price sensitivity, the cost of driving down, WhatsApp everywhere, and language models that now cheaply turn a free-text list into a priced, store-split pick list.
- **A possible secret.** No supermarket sees what a whole village buys, item by item, every day. That aggregated demand is leverage for direct or wholesale terms. The data, not the promotions, is the asset.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The edge belongs to the supplier.** Multi-buy offers are Tawfir's pricing decision, and one buyer pooling a village to hit "buy 3" is what retailers cap per customer or end. Asking Tawfir's owner for a direct deal also shows him Amchit's demand; nothing stops Tawfir, or any supermarket, running its own village pickup. Savings rented from a supplier are not a moat.
2. **Nothing the customer sees is hard to copy.** To the buyer this is a WhatsApp number, a car and a bag; the software is invisible. The mini-market, a neighbour who drives down daily, or a delivery app can copy it by month six, and two operators in one thin-margin village compete the fee away. In ten years trust and density may hold; the arbitrage will not. Whether delivery apps or supermarket delivery already reach Amchit, and at what fee, is open (/founder-competitors).
3. **The licensing plan sells the least defensible part.** For a one-village operator the software is not ten times better than WhatsApp plus a spreadsheet. Licensees mostly do not exist yet and are fragmented and short of cash. Licensed neighbours become rivals for the same promotions, speeding up the caps in risk 1. The price agent rests on scraping supermarket sites that can change, block him, or differ from the shelf. "Here is the whole system" publishes the playbook and keeps none of the moat.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- When Tawfir sees your volume, why would it supply you rather than serve Amchit itself?
- Against the household's own trip down and the mini-market, what is ten times better: price, time, or something else?
- What do you know about how Amchit buys that no supermarket knows?
- How many households are in Amchit, what share must order weekly to fill a run, and do the numbers survive if every multi-buy is capped at one per customer? (/founder-consumer, /founder-cfo)
- Who exactly would license this? Do informal buying runs already exist nearby, would they pay, and why not just use WhatsApp? (/founder-competitors)
- Why rent the software to villages you could own and add to your own buying volume?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **A supply price that does not depend on public promotions:** written direct or wholesale terms from Tawfir or a distributor, checked by /founder-cfo. If it only works on retail multi-buys, this vote becomes PASS.
2. **Density proven in Amchit first:** repeat-ordering households at or above the per-run break-even /founder-cfo computes, held for a set period, before any second village.
3. **Licensing deferred and redesigned:** none until he runs two adjacent villages himself. Then licensees buy through his consolidated purchasing and he earns on network volume, so each village adds buying power to one network instead of creating a rival. The moat is the buying network; the software is how it is delivered.
4. **A competitor map** (/founder-competitors) showing no one already serves Amchit's grocery run at a comparable price.
