# Monopoly lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small market it could own first.** One village, one pickup point, one daily run. The lens wants exactly this: a big share of something tiny. The founder lives there, so the first customers can arrive through WhatsApp and word of mouth for close to nothing.
- **Density is a real local scale effect.** A driver run costs about the same whether it carries few orders or many, so every extra Amchit household lowers the cost per order. The first operator to reach density in a village leaves a second entrant splitting thin volume. That is the one structural advantage here.
- **Timing.** Post-2019 price sensitivity, the cost of driving down to the coast, WhatsApp as the default channel, and language models that can now turn a free-text WhatsApp list into a priced, store-split pick list cheaply. The last one is a genuine "why now" for the software.
- **A possible secret.** No supermarket sees what a whole village buys, item by item, every day. That aggregated demand is leverage for direct or wholesale terms. The data, not the promotions, is the asset.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The edge belongs to the supplier.** Multi-buy offers are Tawfir's pricing decision. One buyer pooling a village to hit "buy 3" is what retailers cap per customer or end. Worse, asking Tawfir's owner for a direct deal shows him Amchit's demand, and nothing stops Tawfir, or any supermarket, from running its own village pickup. Savings rented from a supplier are not a moat.
2. **Nothing the customer sees is hard to copy.** To the buyer this is a WhatsApp number, a car and a bag at a pickup point. The village mini-market, a neighbour who already drives down daily, or a delivery app can copy it by month six; the software is invisible to them. Groceries are a thin-margin commodity, so two operators in one village compete the fee away. In ten years, local trust and density may hold; the price arbitrage will not. Whether delivery apps or supermarket delivery already reach Amchit, and at what fee, is open (/founder-competitors).
3. **The licensing plan sells the least defensible part.** For a one-village operator the software is not ten times better than WhatsApp plus a spreadsheet. The licensees mostly do not exist yet (someone must first decide to run grocery runs), are fragmented and short of cash, and would lean on a solo developer for support. Licensing neighbouring villages creates rivals for the same supplier promotions, speeding up the caps in risk 1. The price agent rests on scraping 5 or 6 supermarket sites that can change, block him, or differ from the shelf. "Here is the whole system" publishes the playbook while keeping none of the moat.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- When Tawfir sees your volume, why would it supply you rather than serve Amchit itself? What do you give Tawfir that it cannot get alone?
- Against the household's own trip down and the mini-market, what is ten times better: price, time, or something else?
- What do you know about how Amchit buys that no supermarket knows?
- How many households are in Amchit, what share must order weekly to fill a run, and do the numbers survive if every multi-buy is capped at one per customer? (/founder-consumer, /founder-cfo)
- Who exactly would license this? Do informal buying runs already exist in nearby villages, how many, would they pay, and why would they not just use WhatsApp? (/founder-competitors)
- Why rent the software to villages you could own and add to your own buying volume?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **A supply price that does not depend on public promotions:** written direct or wholesale terms from Tawfir or a distributor, checked against margin by /founder-cfo. If the model only works on retail multi-buys, this vote becomes PASS.
2. **Density proven in Amchit first:** repeat-ordering households at or above the per-run break-even /founder-cfo computes, held for a set period, before any second village.
3. **Licensing deferred and redesigned:** none until he runs at least two adjacent villages himself. When it comes, licensees buy through his consolidated purchasing and he earns on network volume, so each new village adds buying power to one network instead of creating a rival. The moat is the buying network; the software is how it is delivered.
4. **A competitor map** (/founder-competitors) showing no one already serves Amchit's grocery run at a comparable price.
