# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Buyers are easy to reach.** It is one village, orders come in on WhatsApp and collection is at one pickup point, so finding customers costs almost nothing. Of the lens's four market tests, this is the one the idea clearly passes.
- **The urgency is real.** The 2 p.m. cut-off is a true deadline, so nothing has to be faked to make people order today.
- **The mechanism is real.** Pooled orders can reach multi-buy prices that one household cannot. That is a genuine source of value, not only a claim.
- **It needs little capital.** Software costs nothing (the founder builds it), and nothing is bought until orders are in. If buyers pay before the shop, there is no stock and no working-capital hole.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It is a commodity offer with an unproven price gap.** The whole promise is "cheaper". The lens warns most against exactly this. The money to share comes from multi-buy discounts plus price gaps between stores. Out of that come the driver, fuel, bags, the pickup point, cash handling and the fee. That money could be close to zero. The AI price agent makes this worse: every extra store saves a little on one item but adds a stop and driver time, and the plan does not count that cost. If the fee buyers accept is less than the cost of one order, the business loses money on every bag. Nobody knows the basket size, the real saving or the fee people would accept (open questions: /founder-competitors for real shelf prices and offers, /founder-consumer for the fee, /founder-cfo for cost per order).
2. **The pain is weak in the launch village.** Amchit is right next to Jbeil, so "going down to the city" is a short drive for most of its residents. Many already do it, and the village shop covers top-ups. The pain is likely bigger in villages further inland, but limited cash means only one village can be served. Households tight on money since 2019 like savings but resist fees, which squeezes the same thin gap from both sides.
3. **Likelihood and effort: buyers have no reason to believe it will work.** Buyers give up choosing their own produce and meat. Free-text WhatsApp orders invite mistakes, and out-of-stock items need substitutions nobody has agreed. The buyer still has to go to the pickup point. There is no proof and no guarantee yet. In a village, one bad batch spreads fast. Separately, the supply plan depends on Tawfir: its offers may have per-customer limits, and the owner may simply say no.

The software (dashboard, driver view, agent) helps the operator deliver; the buyer never sees it. Selling it to other villages later depends on proving it works here first.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Name 10 Amchit households who would send you their list next week. Have you asked them?
- What is in a typical weekly basket, and how much of it is on multi-buy offers at Tawfir?
- On one real basket, what is the saving after driver time and fuel?
- Do buyers pay before you shop? Who takes the loss when a bag is never picked up?
- What happens when the tomatoes are bad or an item is out of stock?
- Who drives, and what does an hour of their time cost?
- Why would an Amchit resident not just drive the few minutes to Jbeil?
- Who would still choose you if you doubled the fee? (Older people, families without a car, people working late?)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Prove the gap on paper first.** Price at least three real baskets two ways: bought alone, and pooled across Tawfir and other stores. Then subtract the cost per order. The gap left over must cover the fee with room to spare (/founder-competitors, then /founder-cfo).
2. **Rebuild the offer around time and effort, not price.** For example: "your city shop, done for you, at city prices or better". Add a written guarantee (a wrong or missing item is refunded or replaced the next day) and print the saving on every receipt. Aim first at the buyers for whom the trip is hardest (/founder-offer).
3. **Pre-sell before building.** Take payment upfront at the cut-off and run 2 to 4 weeks by hand: WhatsApp, a spreadsheet, at most 2 stores. Build the agent and dashboard only after the hand-run numbers hold.
4. **Test the fee with buyers:** a flat fee per order against a percentage of the bill (/founder-consumer).
5. **Pick the launch village by pain, not by address.** Compare Amchit with one inland neighbour before committing.
