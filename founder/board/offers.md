# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Buyers are cheap to reach.** One village, WhatsApp orders and one pickup point mean getting customers costs almost nothing. Of the lens's four market tests, this is the one the idea clearly passes.
- **The urgency is real.** The 2 p.m. cut-off is a true deadline.
- **The mechanism is real.** Pooled orders can reach multi-buy prices that one household cannot.
- **It needs little capital.** The founder builds the software and no stock is held, so if buyers pay before the shop, no cash is tied up.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It is a commodity offer with an unproven price gap.** The whole promise is "cheaper", the position the lens warns against most. The money to share is multi-buy discounts plus price gaps between stores. The driver, fuel, bags, pickup point, cash handling and fee all come out of that, and what is left could be close to zero. The AI agent makes this worse: each extra store saves a little on one item but adds a stop and driver time, which the plan does not count. If buyers will only pay a fee below the cost of one order, every bag loses money. Basket size, savings and the fee are open questions (/founder-competitors, /founder-consumer, /founder-cfo).
2. **The pain is weak in the launch village.** Amchit is right next to Jbeil, so "going down to the city" is a short drive, and the village shop covers top-ups. Inland villages likely feel it more, but limited cash means one village at a time. Households tight on money since 2019 want savings but resist fees, squeezing the gap from both sides.
3. **Buyers have no reason to believe it works, and it still asks effort of them.** They give up choosing their own produce and meat. Free-text WhatsApp orders invite mistakes, there is no agreed rule for out-of-stock items, and buyers still have to go to the pickup point. There is no proof and no guarantee, and in a village one bad batch spreads fast. Tawfir may also limit offers per customer, or refuse.

The dashboard, driver view and agent help the operator; the buyer never sees them. Licensing to other villages depends on proving it works here first.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Name 10 Amchit households who would send you their list next week. Have you asked them?
- How much of a typical basket is on multi-buy offers at Tawfir?
- On one real basket, what is the saving after driver time and fuel?
- Do buyers pay before you shop? Who takes the loss on a bag nobody collects?
- What happens when an item is bad or out of stock?
- Why would an Amchit resident not just drive to Jbeil?
- Who would still choose you at double the fee: older people, families without a car, people who work late?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Prove the gap on paper first.** Price three real baskets bought alone and pooled, then subtract the cost per order. What is left must cover the fee with room to spare (/founder-competitors, /founder-cfo).
2. **Sell time and effort, not price.** For example: "your city shop, done for you, at city prices or better". Add a written guarantee (wrong or missing items refunded or replaced the next day), print the saving on every receipt, and aim first at buyers for whom the trip is hardest (/founder-offer).
3. **Pre-sell before building.** Take payment at the cut-off and run 2 to 4 weeks by hand (WhatsApp, a spreadsheet, at most 2 stores). Build the software only once those numbers hold.
4. **Test the fee:** a flat fee per order against a percentage of the bill (/founder-consumer).
5. **Pick the launch village by pain.** Compare Amchit with one inland neighbour first.
