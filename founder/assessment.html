Six findings that changed the plan

"Tawfir" is Tawfeer MAX Jbeil

It opened on 22 May 2026 in Qartaboun, 5–8 km from Amchit, with permanent piece, quantity and carton prices. Carton prices are open to small businesses with no membership, so ask Tawfeer for service, not discounts. The tier thresholds are published nowhere.

Amchit is the wrong first market

Its centre is 5–6 minutes from Spinneys, Tawfeer MAX and Le Charcutier. The pain is up the hill in Bejjeh, Ghalboun, Aabaydat and Kfoun, 20–25 minutes away, where a trip costs about $6.90 in fuel and time, against $2.60 from Amchit.

Comparing 5–6 stores saves 3–6%

On 12 staples, the cheapest single store came to $77.04 and the cheapest store for each item to $74.42. Tawfeer's own bundles alone came to $74.77. Every extra store adds a stop and a split bag.

Scraping is the wrong data source

Spinneys' terms allow only personal, non-commercial viewing, and Carrefour blocks automated access. Your own till receipts, Tawfeer's weekly price list, or a data licence from SoukCompare (daily prices for five nearby chains) do the job legally.

Only car-less households buy

6 of the 8 simulated buyers across both panels had no car at home. They are buying access, not a discount: on the base bag they pay about $0.15 more than Tawfeer's shelf price, in exchange for the trip they don't make.

Nobody pools village orders here

No one in the Jbeil district runs anything like this. The fastest copiers are the village mini-market and Tawfeer itself. So make the mini-market your pickup host, and Tawfeer your partner.

Your questions, answered

Subscription, or a percentage of the invoice?

Neither, to start. Charge a visible flat fee per bag ($1.25, and test $1.50), plus half of the bulk saving, shown on every slip. A percentage punishes the families who buy most and reads like a hidden markup. A subscription only works once households order every week, so test a ~$4-a-month club after three months of data.

The AI agent?

Keep AI where it saves your time. It can read messy WhatsApp lists and voice notes in Lebanese Arabic, Arabizi and French (about $0.0004–$0.008 an order), and turn receipt photos into your own price history. Drop the scraping agent: it gains 3–6%, carries terms-of-use risk and adds stops. Voice notes still need a read-back, since speech-to-text gets about one word in four wrong in Lebanese Arabic.

The dashboard and driver view?

Yes, after the two hand-run test runs. The design is in product-tech.md: a Postgres data model with a tenant ID from day one, an offline driver app, and receipts as the price database. Running costs are about $13–$49 a month.

Selling or renting the software to other villages?

Not yet. A village run earns its operator roughly $100–$130 a month, while software for micro-operators costs $50–$350 a month and loses about 4% of its customers every month. They can't pay what supporting them would cost you. The better route is to run two neighbouring villages yourself, then offer the channel to a retailer (Tawfeer has 41 branches and a 500-store target), or let new villages buy through your one consolidated account.

Approaching Tawfeer?

Go as a channel, not a discount hunter. Ask for the tier thresholds in writing, an invoice account, pre-picking of an order sent by 08:00, weekly settlement, and permission to print their shelf prices on your slips. Offer an 8-week, 4-village pilot with numbers every two weeks. The script, with an opening in Lebanese Arabic, is in tawfeer-pitch.md.
